1.0 // PROBLEM

"We need to adopt AI". But where?

Managers hear about AI everywhere. Competitors are adopting something, staff are overloaded, hiring keeps getting more expensive. But between "we need AI" and "AI adopted" sits a question nobody answers: which specific tasks in your department should go to AI, and which should not.

$ task: weekly report for management
> 3 hours of manual copying from CRM to Excel
$ task: replies to routine client emails
> 40% of the workday, same template every time

Top-down adoption without an audit always ends the same way: a tool gets bought, employees don't use it, zero effect. The cause: nobody looked at what people's workday actually consists of. The audit starts exactly there.

2.0 // SCOPE OF WORK

What the audit includes

I take the department through documenting its work processes and turn observations into a calculation. Stages:

COMM

Team communication

Before the start: how management presents the audit to staff. Skip this step and people game the data, making the whole calculation worthless.

PHOTO

Work-week snapshot

Each employee logs their tasks for a week: what, how often, how many minutes. Simple format, 5 minutes a day.

MAP

Department task map

All tasks consolidated and broken into sub-processes. You see what people actually do and where the duplication is.

MATRX

Automation matrix

Each sub-process: automatable / partially / no. With reasoning: cost, risk, error criticality.

CALC

Freed-capacity calculation

Hours per month per role, implementation cost, payback period. Numbers you can take to the director.

PLAN

Final report and plan

What to implement first, second, and what to leave alone. A report walkthrough with the manager is included.

The next step after the audit is an automation specification: technical requirements for specific agents under the ANSS standard. The audit stands on its own: you can take the report to any contractor.

4.0 // ENGAGEMENT INVARIANTS

Rules that are never broken

INV-01

The audit's output is a freed-capacity map, not a layoff list. The decision is yours: cut headcount, take on more orders, or unburden your people.

INV-02

Data is collected with employees' knowledge. The audit is presented to the team as reinforcement. That's not a formality: covert observation produces distorted data and a useless calculation.

INV-03

If the first week shows there's nothing worth automating in the department, I'll say so directly, and you pay only for the express stage.

INV-04

The price is fixed and quoted after a free 30-minute conversation. No hourly billing: you pay for results.
