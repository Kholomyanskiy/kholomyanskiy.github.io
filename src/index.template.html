5.0 // PROCESS

How an implementation project goes

For consulting and training the path is shorter: a free call, then sessions.

A description of the department and the task in two or three sentences. The reply says whether it is worth doing and where to start.
The focus is on real processes: what people do day to day and how long it takes. Result: a task map with an estimate of what can be automated. Audit formats and prices → · Automation payback calculator →
Solution architecture and a precise spec for each task: input, output, rules, what must never happen.
Rolled out one task at a time, accepted by whoever used to do it by hand, until the result is stable.
The solution is handed over with documentation. After that, as needed: one-off consultations or the next stage of automation.
6.0 // WHY I DO THIS

Where this interest comes from

I've always been interested in finding ways to make processes simpler and more convenient. Even before modern AI tools, I kept noticing repetitive actions, unnecessary steps, and awkward processes that could be changed. From everyday things to work systems: if an action repeats regularly, there's usually a way to make it simpler or more efficient.

The same happens in business. Employees perform many repetitive operations every day: searching for information, processing emails, filling out documents, preparing reports, transferring data between systems. I'm interested in finding the places where a process can be made simpler, faster, and more reliable.

My enterprise IT experience taught me to approach this systematically: first understand the task, then describe the process, and only then choose the technology. Today AI has become the next stage of this approach. It lets you build systems that take over routine work and free up time for decisions where a human is genuinely needed.
